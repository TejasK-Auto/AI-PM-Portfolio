# AI Product Manager Portfolio

## Purpose

This portfolio is being set up to show how I approach product work from problem discovery through delivery, launch, measurement, and learning, with a particular focus on products that use AI responsibly.

The portfolio should demonstrate decisions and evidence, not just collect templates. Each case study should make clear what the user problem was, what I learned, what choices I made, what I personally contributed, and how success would be measured.

## Intended audience

The primary audience is product hiring teams and collaborators who want to understand how I:

- Find and validate important customer problems
- Turn research and market context into a focused product strategy
- Define and test solutions before committing to a full build
- Work with design, engineering, analytics, marketing, and other stakeholders
- Make a product measurable, launchable, and sustainable
- Decide when AI is appropriate and design for its risks and limitations

## Portfolio principles

1. **Start with a real user need.** Explain the target user, their context, and the evidence behind the problem.
2. **Show the reasoning.** Make assumptions, alternatives, trade-offs, risks, and non-goals visible.
3. **Connect artifacts to decisions.** A PRD, prototype, or roadmap should answer a specific question in the case.
4. **Measure outcomes.** Define a North Star or primary outcome, supporting metrics, and guardrails. Clearly distinguish actual results from estimates and targets.
5. **Treat AI as a product choice, not a badge.** Compare an AI approach with simpler alternatives and explain the model, UX, evaluation, privacy, safety, and cost trade-offs.
6. **Be accurate about ownership.** State whether work was individual, collaborative, academic, proposed, or shipped, and identify my contribution.

## Recommended case-study structure

Each case can use this narrative, adapting the depth to the project:

1. **Snapshot:** product, role, timeline, team, and project status
2. **Problem and audience:** who has the problem, in what context, and why it matters
3. **Evidence:** interviews, observations, usability tests, analytics, market research, or clearly labeled assumptions
4. **Opportunity:** user value, business value, feasibility, alternatives, and prioritization
5. **Strategy:** target segment, positioning, value proposition, success definition, and what is out of scope
6. **Solution and validation:** user flows, wireframes, prototype, experiments, findings, and changes made
7. **Product definition and delivery:** PRD, stories, acceptance criteria, dependencies, risks, and delivery plan
8. **Business and launch plan:** business model, pricing logic, channels, GTM, and lifecycle considerations
9. **Measurement:** metric tree, baseline, targets, guardrails, instrumentation, and evaluation plan
10. **Outcomes and reflection:** results if measured; otherwise expected outcomes, open questions, and next steps
11. **For AI features:** model-fit rationale, evaluation approach, human controls, failure states, and responsible-AI safeguards

Begin with two or three complete, well-supported cases rather than many shallow ones. A balanced set could include a discovery-to-delivery product case, a strategy/GTM case, and an AI product case. The HireMate capstone in the coursework is a possible case only if it reflects my own work; its academic context and my specific contribution should be stated.

## Publication checklist

- Remove or anonymize personal, confidential, and identifiable research information.
- Label hypotheses, projections, simulated results, and measured outcomes accurately.
- Cite the evidence behind important product decisions.
- Use only artifacts I have permission to publish.
- Explain AI involvement, evaluation, limitations, and user safeguards in plain language.
